Overview · the best rational approximations

Every real number hides a staircase of integers. The continued fraction \([a_0; a_1, a_2, \dots]\) unfolds a number into its arithmetically optimal rational approximations — the convergents \(p_k/q_k\). Their power-series cousins, the Padé approximants, do the same for functions: they replace a truncated Taylor series with a ratio of polynomials that converges far faster and even past the radius of convergence.

This page is interactive. Expand real numbers below, watch the convergent errors collapse, compare Padé against Taylor on the same plot, and read how all of it ties into my summer research on Hadamard gaps.

II · Padé approximants

A Padé approximant \([m/n]\) of a power series \(f(x)=\sum c_k x^k\) is the rational function \(\dfrac{P_m(x)}{Q_n(x)}\) (with \(Q_n(0)=1\)) whose Taylor series matches \(f\) through order \(x^{m+n}\). It is, in a precise sense, the continued-fraction idea applied to functions. Choose a series and the \([m/n]\) orders, then compare against the truncated Taylor polynomial of the same total degree.

III · Continued fractions, Padé & Hadamard gaps

The thread connecting both halves of this page is rational approximation as a microscope on convergence. A continued fraction reorganizes a real number so that the next integer \(a_{k}\) tells you exactly how much accuracy the next convergent buys. A Padé table does the same for a function: the location of its poles reveals the singularities of \(f\) that a Taylor series can only feel as a shrinking radius of convergence.

A Hadamard gap series (lacunary series) is one of the form \(\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{k} c_k z^{n_k}\) where the exponents grow geometrically, \(n_{k+1}/n_k \ge \lambda > 1\). The Hadamard gap theorem says such a series has its circle of convergence as a natural boundary: it cannot be analytically continued past \(|z|=R\) anywhere. The function is, in a strong sense, trapped inside its disk.

In my summer-intern research I studied Padé approximants of Hadamard-gap series. The question: if a function's Taylor series is mostly zeros (a gap structure), what do its Padé approximants do? The poles of the \([m/n]\) approximants cluster on the natural boundary, tracing out the circle the function can never cross. Where ordinary series merely fail to converge, the Padé denominators \(Q_n\) actively map the wall — their zeros accumulate densely on \(|z|=R\). The gap structure that makes the boundary natural is exactly what organizes the pole distribution, and the spacing of the gaps controls how the convergents (now in the function sense) line up — a direct echo of how the partial quotients \(a_k\) control the convergents of a real number above.

In short: continued fractions and Padé approximants are the same instinct — fold the object into a sequence of best rational pieces — applied to numbers and to analytic functions respectively. Hadamard gaps are where that instinct meets the hard limit of analytic continuation, and the Padé poles draw the limit for you.
