A cubic Bézier, built by repeated linear interpolation

Drag the four control points. The curve is traced by the de Casteljau algorithm: nest lerps between points, and the single surviving point at parameter t rides the curve. Move the t slider to watch the scaffolding collapse onto the curve. This is the primitive under every vector graphic — the thing you drag a handle on in Illustrator.

From a noisy hand-drawn stroke to a clean curve

Draw a freehand stroke on the canvas. It captures dozens of jittery sample points. Then watch the Ramer–Douglas–Peucker simplification throw away points that don't matter (controlled by ε), and a least-squares Bézier fit replace the polyline with smooth curves — exactly the pipeline behind "pencil → vector path."

RDP: keep the point of max perpendicular distance > ε; recurse on each half

Raster → Vector: tracing a bitmap into paths

A tiny black-and-white bitmap (paint your own below). The tracer: (1) thresholds to a binary image, (2) walks the boundary between black and white with the Moore neighbour-tracing contour algorithm, (3) simplifies the contour with RDP, and (4) emits an SVG path. This is image-to-vector in miniature — the heart of "Image Trace".
